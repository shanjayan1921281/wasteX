# Industrial Textile Byproducts & Fibre Regeneration Standards (WasteXchange KB-TX-01)
**Authoritative Reference**: WasteXchange Circular Textile Taskforce & Industrial Fiber Recycling Protocol
**Document Code**: `KB-TX-01`
**Category**: `Textile`
**Keywords**: Comber noil, spinning sweeps, selvedge cuts, regenerated rotor yarn, garnetting, cotton cellulose

## 1. Material Classification & Physical Attributes
Industrial textile waste generated in yarn spinning, fabric weaving, and garment fabrication is categorized by staple length, twist level, and synthetic admixture:
- **Grade A Comber Noil**: 100% combed virgin cotton fibers extracted during the combing phase of fine yarn manufacturing (staple length 12-18mm). Contains negligible seed coats or trash.
- **Grade B Loom Selvedge & Cuttings**: Twisted selvage edges with 70-85% cotton and 15-30% synthetic sizing agents or elastane/polyester warp threads. Requires mechanical pin-cylinder garnetting to unbind yarn twist.
- **Grade C Mill Sweeps & Card Fly**: Shorter staple fibers (<10mm) collected from suction collectors and filtration units; higher particulate and lubricating oil micro-droplets.

## 2. Technical Quality Thresholds & Permissible Tolerances
- **Moisture Threshold**: Equilibrium moisture regain must not exceed 8.0% (standard oven-dry testing ASTM D2495). Batches with moisture > 9.5% are subject to bacterial rotting, mold development, and severe carding cylinder wire clothing jam.
- **Contamination Criteria**:
  - Non-fibrous particulate matter (dust, seed hull, sand): < 1.5% by weight.
  - Foreign tramp material (polypropylene baling twine, metal clips, grease): Strictly 0.0% tolerance. PP twine melts into un-dyeable streaks in finished recycled fabric.
- **Purity & Fiber Length Distribution**:
  - Comber noil with > 85% fibers above 12mm commands top spot prices in open-end rotor spinning mills.

## 3. Circular Recovery & Industrial Pathways
1. **Mechanical Yarn Regeneration (Recycling - Primary)**:
   - *Process*: Mechanical garnetting with progressive fine-wire beating rollers, blending with 20-30% carrier fiber, followed by open-end rotor spinning (Ne 10s to Ne 20s).
   - *End Products*: Secondary denim warp yarn, knitted industrial gloves, mop yarns, canvas tarpaulins.
   - *Technical Constraint*: Fiber length is shortened by 15-20% per mechanical cycle; fiber cannot be perpetually recycled without virgin blending.
2. **Acoustic & Thermal Insulation Nonwovens (Recovery - Secondary)**:
   - *Process*: Air-laid aerodynamic web formation coupled with low-melt bicomponent polyester bonding fibers, passing through a 160°C convection oven.
   - *End Products*: Automotive dashboard acoustic dampeners, architectural insulation batts, appliance sound barriers.
   - *Advantage*: High tolerance for mixed colors and shorter staple lengths.
3. **Cellulose Micro-Powder & Chemical Upcycling (Upcycling - Specialty)**:
   - *Process*: Acid hydrolysis or ultra-fine mechanical milling into 50-micron microcrystalline cellulose.
   - *End Products*: Biopolymer compounding additives, luxury embossed archival stationery, pharmaceutical tablet binders.
4. **Direct Wipe & Secondary Rag (Direct Reuse)**:
   - *Process*: Cutting, magnetic scanning for needle detection, and automated shrink-packing into 10kg lint-free wipe packs.

## 4. Market Demand Reality & Price Sensitivity
- **Actual Market Demand**: Sustained High Demand across textile manufacturing belts (Tiruppur, Coimbatore, Panipat, Ahmedabad, Surat). Escalating virgin cotton prices drive mills to integrate up to 40% comber noil into standard rotor counts.
- **Critical Caution**: Blended dyed poly-cotton knitwear clippings (especially 50/50 PC) face 40% lower buyer demand than un-dyed white single-component cotton due to color-sorting labor overhead.
